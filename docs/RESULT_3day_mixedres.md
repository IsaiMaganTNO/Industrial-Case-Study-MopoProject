## 2. Key changes in NL+BE vs vre15
- **Industry output is unchanged.** Production by sector matches vre15 within 0.5 in every year, so demand is met the same way. Only the routes change.
- **Investment differences (sum of units, NL+BE):**
  - Lost: REF-SMR-MEA −63.7, bio-boiler −7.6, nuclear-3 −5.87 GW, DAC −4.0, (H2)PEA −1.9, CCGT-H2 −1.4, oil-eng −1.7.
  - Gained: gas-boiler +8.4, CCGT+CC +8.3, bio-diges-up+CC +5.0, bioST +3.0, (NG)PEA-MEA +2.0.
  - So the shift is from H2, nuclear and DAC inside NL+BE towards gas with CCS and biomethane.
- **DAC moves out of NL+BE.** vre15 builds 4.0 units in NLC2 (35/29 Mt in 2040/2050). The mixed run builds 9.96 in NO and 1.45 in DK instead, and none in NL/BE.
- **Hydrogen:** LH2 imports are already at their cap (42.5 TWh) from 2040 in both runs. In 2030 the mixed run imports 35.5 vs 9.7 TWh. PEM output in 2050 is 3.2 vs 4.4 TWh.
- **Electricity:** gas (CCGT+CC) supplies about 71 TWh in 2050 (BE 62.7, NL 8.5) and new nuclear is zero. BE+NL offshore falls (BE 35→21, NL 158→139 TWh). NL becomes a net exporter (−31 TWh); BE imports +12.7 TWh.

## 3. Why gas replaces nuclear and H2 (diagnosis)
1. **Gas supply is effectively unlimited and cheap.** NO extraction is 1.1–1.3 PWh/yr (capacity 3,931 GWh/d) with vom ≈ 0.015, and `limited_fossil-CH4` is inert. The LNG cap (0.5) does not bind, because pipeline gas NO→DE/UK/DK→BE/NL reaches NL+BE anyway.
2. **The CO2 budget is shared across the 6 countries.** The budget (1146/254.7/0 Mt, atmosphere level 3140.7 = 1146 Mt/365) can be met at the system level. Cheap sinks are added outside NL+BE: CO2-storage-DK holds 243 Mt in 2050, and DAC in NO/DK about 100 Mt. These offset gas-CCS residual emissions in NL+BE. In vre15, NL+BE had to cover their own budget, which forced nuclear-3, H2 turbines and DAC in NLC2.
3. **Result:** CCGT+CC plus offsite negative emissions is cheaper than new nuclear (at 0 €/t fuel cost and with no gas volume limit).

## 4. Artefacts / caveats
- **Upstream carbon attribution:** fossil CO2 is booked at the extraction or LNG gate (0.2 t/MWh). As a result, NO shows 263 Mt in 2050 from exported gas, and per-country totals are **not** consumption-based. Use system totals, or re-attribute by net CH4 imports (DE 480, UK 565, DK 112, BE 104, NL 21 TWh).
- **DK CO2 storage of 243 Mt/yr in 2050** (investment 25.5 in 2041) far exceeds announced DK capacity (~40–60 Mt/yr by 2050). The DK storage potential needs a cap.
- **DAC at 100 Mt/yr in NO/DK** is optimistic. Check the cost and potential parameters.
- **UK:** no new onshore wind (cap 25.4 GW unused) and 315 TWh gas CCGT+CC in 2050. This is consistent with cheap NO pipeline gas and unrestricted CCS; UK gas extraction is missing (known gap).
- No external (FR, LU, etc.) free supply: the transit nodes balance.

## 5. Recommendations / next runs
1. **Gas-limit sensitivity:** activate the six-country `fossil-CH4` volume limit (1,102,262 / 440,905 / 220,452 GWh — verify the /365 scaling first), and/or cap NO extraction.
2. Cap CO2 storage injection per country (DK in particular) and review the DAC potential.
3. Report emissions on a consumption basis (re-attribute upstream gate CO2).
4. Consider per-country nuclear caps (BE 6 / NL 4–5 / UK 6–13 GW, per the external workbook); this needs a schema change.

Conclusion: the NL+BE results from the isolated `vre15` run are **not robust** to coupling with neighbours. Under the current assumptions, the larger system replaces new NL+BE nuclear and H2 with Norwegian gas + CCS and offsite CO2 sinks. This is driven mainly by the uncapped gas supply and the uncapped DK storage and DAC.
